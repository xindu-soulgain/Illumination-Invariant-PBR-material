Chord: Chain of Rendering Decomposition for PBR Material Estimation from Generated Texture Images

A two-stage framework that generates tileable textures and sequentially estimates SVBRDF channels through chained diffusion-based decomposition.

SIGGRAPH Asia 2025

Illumination Invariant PBR material

Du Xin*, Xu Maoyuan*
Ubisoft LaForge
2026
*Indicates Equal Contribution

Abstract

We investigate illumination-invariant material decomposition by learning material representations that disentangle intrinsic properties from illumination-dependent appearance. Our current focus is a weakly supervised representation learning framework that combines physically rendered supervision with DINO-inspired self-distillation, treating renderings of the same material under different illumination conditions as positive pairs. Given geometry, we aim to recover physically meaningful material properties under a Cook-Torrance BRDF, progressively estimating basecolor, normal, metallic, and roughness. Mitsuba 3-based differentiable rendering further enables physics-consistent supervision and controlled illumination interventions at scale. The current experiments focus on learning the illumination-invariant representation using the MatSynth SVBRDF dataset conditions on gemoetry infomation, a subsequent stage will jointly recover geometry alongside material, removing the need for external depth conditioning.

Our approach shares with IntrinsicAnything the goal of resolving the inherent ambiguity between material and illumination through learned material priors, as well as the use of physically based rendering for material recovery. However, while IntrinsicAnything learns diffusion priors of albedo and specular components to regularize inverse rendering under unknown illumination, our approach seeks to remove illumination dependence directly at the representation-learning level. In this sense, we aim to learn a representation in which the same material remains consistent across varying illumination, so that, once geometry is provided, material decomposition can converge toward physically meaningful and perceptually plausible albedo and BRDF properties.

The current prototype has several known limitations that reflect its early stage. Because each training case currently uses a single, randomly sampled environment lighting condition rather than the multi-illumination positive pairs described above, the model has not yet fully learned to disentangle illumination from material identity. This appears as visible color shift on cases such as the manhole cover, human face, and car examples, where the recovered albedo is noticeably less faithful to the object's true material color than 3DAT (our Qwen-Image-based finetune). This is direct evidence for why the self-distillation stage is necessary, and we are actively extending training to multi-illumination positive pairs to address it. Separately, we observe that shadow-related low-frequency illumination is removed more effectively than specular highlights (e.g., the elephant example retains visible specular residue after shadow removal), suggesting that diffuse and specular components may require distinct treatment under the Cook-Torrance decomposition, a direction we are investigating as part of the staged basecolor/metallic/roughness estimation. I'm happy to discuss these failure modes and our current thinking in more detail if useful.
